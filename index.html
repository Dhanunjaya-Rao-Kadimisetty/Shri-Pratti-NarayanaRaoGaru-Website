Welcome to the official tribute website for Sri Pratti Narayana Rao Garu, the first person to translate Sai Satcharitra into Telugu in 1953. His contributions to Sai literature and spirituality remain unparalleled.

Introduction to Sri Pratti Narayana Rao Garu

Sri Pratti Narayana Rao Garu was a noble scholar, devout seeker, and revered educator from Vizianagaram, Andhra Pradesh. Dedicated to spiritual wisdom and literary excellence, he served as a respected teacher at a modern tutorial college before dedicating his life entirely to the service of Bhagavan Sri Shirdi Sai Baba. His exemplary devotion and scholarly devotion laid the foundational cornerstone for Telugu-speaking devotees to experience the life and nectar-like teachings of Sai Baba in their mother tongue.

His Contribution to Telugu Sai Satcharitra

Sri Pratti Narayana Rao stands immortalized in Sai literature as the pioneer who translated the divine Sri Sai Satcharitra into Telugu. Originally penned in Marathi verse by Sri Govind Raghunath Dabholkar (Hemadpanth) and subsequently rendered into English by Sri Nagesh Vasudev Gunaji, the sacred work found its authentic, devotional Telugu expression through Pratti Narayana Rao's tireless efforts. His translation captured the spiritual essence, philosophical depth, and tender compassion of Sai Baba's leelas with poetic clarity and profound fidelity.

Historical Background of the Telugu Translation

During the early 1950s, Sri Pratti Narayana Rao Garu felt an intense spiritual calling that drew him to Shirdi, where he made the holy precincts his permanent home. Residing in the sanctified atmosphere of Shirdi, he formed a deep spiritual bond with Sri Sivanesan Swamiji, spending countless hours in profound contemplation at the Chavadi. It was within the sanctified womb of Chavadi that Pratti Narayana Rao sat and undertook the translation of Sri Sai Satcharitra. Inspired by divine grace, he frequently composed more than a chapter a day, completing the entire holy book in just 40 days.

Prior to publishing the work, he performed three crore recitations of Sri Sai Naama Japa as a sacred Naama Yajna for self-purification. The historic first edition of the Telugu Sai Satcharitra was ceremoniously released on 19th May 1953. The very first copy was reverently presented to Smt. Dukki Sai Leelamma Garu, marking the dawn of a new era for Sai devotees across Andhra Pradesh.

His Legacy in Sai Literature

Following the publication, Sri Pratti Narayana Rao Garu personally undertook Parayana (devotional reading) of the Telugu Sri Sai Satcharitra across 26 sacred kshetras and pilgrimage centers throughout India, including Kashi, Shirdi, Kolhapur, Srisailam, Rameshwaram, Tirupati, and Pandharpur. Through his impassioned discourses and steadfast Sai Prachara, he helped spread the divine message of Shirdi Sai Baba far and wide. Decades later, his seminal Telugu translation remains the cherished companion of millions of devotees worldwide, preserved across successive 10th, 20th, and 30th editions.
